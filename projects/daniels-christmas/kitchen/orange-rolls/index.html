Grandma Moore's Orange Rolls

These were the orange rolls that my grandmother made for every holiday. She would usually stay with us when they visited California. Usually the first question she asked as she walked in the door was if my mother had all her baking ingredients ready. The three things I most remember her making were peanut butter cookies with Brachs candy stars in the center, homemade doughnuts, and of course the orange rolls. The orange rolls were torture because they were SO fragrant but we weren’t allowed samples because they were all saved for Christmas dinner, and even then, they were carefully rationed. If we had Christmas dinner at my Uncle’s house, she would pack up the orange rolls for dinner, and some extra for my uncle’s household. Something my brother viewed as extremely unfair since our cousins hadn’t had to endure a house full of the scent of fresh baked forbidden happiness. One year my grandmother remembered the orange rolls for dinner, but forgot the special package of extras for my uncle. This brought my brother much glee and I’m not sure my uncle was ever convinced that there wasn’t some foul play involved there. It was a scandal that lasted decades.

For years we begged Grandma to write down the recipe. By the time she did she had Parkinson’s and her handwriting was shaky. She had also been baking orange rolls for so many years, she did it more by intuition at that point, so her instructions were pretty vague. I have no idea how much total flour goes in to the rolls, for example. The instructions keep saying “add more.” Baking these is an art, and is not entirely unlike a technical challenge from the Great British Baking Show. But they are SOOO good when they turn out right!


  • 2 packs dry yeast, softened in ½ cup warm water
  • 1½ cups milk, scalded

Add:

  • ½ cup sugar
  • ¼ cup margarine, cup up in pieces
  • 1 tsp salt

When cooled, add:

  • 2 beaten eggs, 1 ½ cups flour and beat well.

Add:

  • ¾ cup orange juice
  • Grated rind of one orange

Add more flour and turn out on a floured board and knead in more flour. Put in a greased mixing bowl and cover with saran wrap. Keep in a warm place to rise for 45 minutes or more, until real light.

Punch down, roll out ½ “ thick and cut in circles. Place on a greased cookie sheet and let rise again (for about 20 minutes).

Bake at 350 for 20 minutes or so.
