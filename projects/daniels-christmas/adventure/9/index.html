You sling your backpack over your shoulder, put on your mask and head out the door. Before too long the lights of the city are well behind you and you are glad to enjoy the open skies and the broad landscapes that lay before you.

The weather is getting colder and you have switched your street shoes to your boots so your feet are nice and toasty. Your mask is keeping your face warm. Unfortunately, you left behind the hat and gloves your Grandma knit you and your fingers are getting stiff and cold. You jam them in your pocket and keep going.

As you walk you hear a distant and curious hum. You wonder what it could possibly be. It doesn’t sound like anything you have heard before. As you turn a corner you come across an angry swarm of murder hornets. Before you know it, you are surrounded and they are stinging even through your clothing.

You try to defend yourself, but your hands are stuck in your pockets and you can’t get them out in time to shoo away the attackers. You feel your throat constrict as an allergic reaction sets in. You gasp for air. Your last thoughts are filled with regret that you underestimated the power of grandma’s love and how it would have freed you to fend for yourself in this dangerous world. Sorry, Grandma!
